00 — THESIS

You cannot advertise a dating app to a girl in Jalgaon. The ad itself is the risk. What you can do is give a hundred of them a private reason to say yes — and then let four lakh people find out second-hand.

Every dating app in India dies on the same number: the male-to-female ratio. Not downloads, not ARPU, not brand love. Ratio. Everything below is engineered backwards from that one number.
00B — IN PLAIN WORDS

Agar sirf ek paragraph padhna ho

Ignore the jargon. This is the whole idea, in the language you'd use at a chai stall.

Dating app pe ladke bahut zyada, ladkiyan bahut kam. Ladkiyan aati hi nahi — kyunki dar lagta hai ki koi dekh lega, ya koi jaan-pehchan wala mil jayega.

To hum app ko sabke liye kholte hi nahi. Pehle sirf 100 ladkiyan andar. Har ek ko ek chhota brass ka sikka. Wahi sikka uski entry hai. Aur wo sikka hum bechte nahi — ladkiyan aapas mein baantti hain.

Hum kuch nahi bechte. Wo us ladki ko deti hai jiski wo guarantee le sakti hai. Ek line: "ispe mera naam hai, sirf unko dena jinko tu jaanti hai."

Parlour mein free photo khichwao, verified badge mil gaya. Sikka hi uska ID hai. Coin nahi, to profile nahi.

5

Ek party — sirf ladkiyon ki

Rooftop, mehendi, music. Baaki 40 sikka stage se baant do. 100 ladkiyan ek room mein = newspaper wale khud aa jayenge, paisa nahi lagega.

Ab ladke

Wo register nahi kar sakte. Waiting room mein baithte hain. Andar aane ke liye kisi ladki ka code chahiye. Har ladki ke paas 3 code = 300 ladke.

Kyun kaam karega

  • Ladki ko kuch prove nahi karna. Sikka purse mein, kisi ko pata nahi.
  • Usko pata hai andar kaun hai — kyunki uski dost ne bulaya.
  • Ladke line mein hain, isliye usko reply milta hai. Reply mile to wo rukti hai.

Sabse chhoti line

Hum app nahi bech rahe. Hum 100 ladkiyon ko sikka de rahe hain, aur wo sikka unki dost ko dena hai. Ladke baad mein aayenge — aur tabhi jab koi ladki haan kahe.

02 — DIAGNOSIS

Why a Jalgaon girl will not install this

Four blockers, in order of severity. Note that none of them are "she doesn't want romance." She wants it more than you do. She just can't afford to be seen wanting it.

1

Visibility risk

In a town of 5 lakh, a phone screen is a public document. A cousin sees the icon. A friend sees the Instagram follow. The cost of being spotted is not embarrassment — it's a year of family commentary.

2

Recognition risk

Metro anonymity doesn't exist here. Every profile is somebody's neighbour, somebody's cousin, somebody's tuition sir. A bad match isn't a bad date — it's a story that circulates for six months.

3

Zero social proof

"Who else is on it?" is the first question, and the only acceptable answer is a name she knows. No ad can answer it. A friend can answer it in one sentence.

4

Marriage-market cost

Being publicly "on a dating app" reads as a discount on her rishta value. This is unfair and completely real, and it means the product must be private before it is public.

Design consequence: the acquisition channel cannot require her to be seen wanting it. Which rules out Instagram ads, campus banners, and "download our dating app" reels. It does not rule out a friend quietly handing her a coin.
03 — THE IDEA

शंभर नाणी — The Hundred Coins

One hundred numbered brass coins. One hundred verified women. Zero men until the hundredth coin is placed. The coin is the invite, the key, the status object, and the ad — all at once.

STEP 01
Ten Coin Keepers
Hand-pick 10 women with reach: mehendi artist, parlour owner, Zumba trainer, wedding photographer, girls'-college senior, boutique owner, tuition madam, nurse, RJ, meme-page admin. Each gets 10 coins.
STEP 02
Ten private gifts
A keeper never "promotes" you. She hands a coin to a girl she'd personally vouch for, in person, with one sentence. That sentence is the entire campaign.
STEP 03
Coin = verified profile
Serial number on the back, QR on the front. Scan → onboarding → free professional photo shoot at a partner parlour → verified badge. No coin, no profile.
STEP 04
Coin Night
Women-only evening at a Jalgaon rooftop. Mehendi, live music, photos, the last 40 coins handed out in the room. This is the press moment — free, because 100 women in one room is a story.
STEP 05
Men unlock
On day 43 the app opens to men — but only with a vouch code from a coin holder. 100 women × 3 codes = 300 men. A curated 1:3 ratio instead of the market's 1:8.

The inversion: in Khandesh, a man gets in only if a woman says yes.

Every dating app makes women the product and men the customer. Flip it once, loudly, and you own the story. A man cannot register — he sits in a public waiting room until a coin holder spends one of her three codes on him. Codes are tied to her account, revocable, and never transferable. She is not the supply. She is the gate.

Why it works on her

  • Private by default. A brass coin in a purse is invisible. Nothing to explain to anyone.
  • Status, not shame. She's #037 of a hundred, not "a girl on a dating app".
  • Social proof built in. The person who gave it to her is the proof.
  • Control. She decides which men even exist in the pool. That has never been offered to her before.
  • Free forever. Unlimited likes, no blur, no paywall on her side. Ever.

Why it works on him

  • Scarcity. 300 seats in a district of 40 lakh. Men chase what is closed.
  • Status. Being vouched means a woman put her name on him. He will behave, because she can revoke.
  • A real chance. At 1:3 he might actually get a reply, which no Jalgaon man has ever experienced on Tinder.
  • A story to tell. "मला एका मुलीने आत घेतलं" is a better chai-stall line than "मी Tinder वर आहे".

05 — THE SUPPORT PLAYS

Four things to run in parallel

None of these are the idea. All of them are force multipliers on it. Do them only after the coins are in motion.

A · Marathi ASO land-grab

Cost: ~₹8,000 and two days. Return: first-page rankings on dozens of terms with literally zero competition — "जळगाव डेटिंग", "खानदेश मॅट्रिमोनी", "धुळे मित्र", "Bhet Khandesh". The Vmobify case study calls vernacular ASO "nearly free distribution". Nobody in your category has done it.

Do this in week 1. It compounds while everything else runs.

B · Parlour & boutique network

12 nodes across the three districts. Mirror sticker + QR + a free profile shoot. Owner earns ₹100 per verified woman. A parlour is the perfect distribution node: high trust, 90-minute dwell time, and the owner talks to 40 women a week. These are your coin distribution points after Coin Night.

₹6,000 setup, ₹100/verified woman. Cheapest trust you will ever buy.

C · Six micro-creators, not one star

5k–50k followers, Marathi, real homes, normal lighting, no studio. Brief them on one thing only: the coin, and the fact that men can't get in. ₹7,000 each. Their job is not installs — it is making the coin recognisable so that when a friend hands one over, it already means something.

₹42,000 · highest-leverage paid spend in the plan.

D · Rickshaw + one hoarding, local humour

Five autos on the Jalgaon–Dhule route, ₹4,000/month each. One board on the ring road. Copy is a joke, not a pitch — Tinder's Northeast playbook proved local humour out-performs product messaging in non-metro. Run for a minimum of 3 months; these markets build memory by repetition, not frequency.

₹40,000 for two months. Not for women — for the men's waiting room.

07 — 90 DAYS

The calendar

Week 1–2 · Build & mint
Coins in hand, product closed
  • Ship the 7 builds above. Nothing else matters this fortnight.
  • Order 100 coins: 40mm brass, gold polish, serial 001–100 on the reverse, QR on the face. Die ₹7,500 + ₹65/coin.
  • Marathi store listing live. Icon and screenshots in Marathi first.
  • Sign 12 parlours. Hand each owner a sticker, a QR, and the ₹100 rate card.
Week 3–4 · The ten keepers
Sixty coins, sixty handshakes
  • Meet all 10 keepers individually. No group briefing, no deck. One conversation each, in person.
  • Give each 10 coins. Say the sentence: "ह्या दहा नाण्यांवर तुझं नाव आहे. ज्यांना तू खरंच ओळखते, त्यांनाच दे."
  • Target: 60 coins placed by day 28. If you're under 40, the keeper model is wrong — go parlour-only and don't look back.
  • Photoshoots running at partner parlours. Every woman leaves with 6 good photos, which she will use everywhere, forever.
Week 5–6 · Coin Night
A hundred women in one room
  • Rooftop buyout, women only, mehendi + live music + photographer. The last 40 coins handed out in the room, on stage, by number.
  • Invite three reporters and one local news channel. You are not pitching an app — you are covering an event.
  • Simultaneously open the men's waiting room. Counter goes live. Men can queue; they cannot enter.
  • Micro-creators post the same week, so the coin is already familiar when the news breaks.
Week 7–12 · Open the gate
300 vouched men, then Dhule
  • Coin holders spend their 3 codes. Men arrive pre-vetted by a woman who knows them.
  • Watch one number only: percentage of women who get a match within 7 days. Below 30% → slow the queue, widen the radius, do not spend more.
  • Rickshaws and hoarding go up now — aimed at men, with the waiting-room joke.
  • At 100/100 in Jalgaon, open Dhule with coins 101–200. Different city, different series, same ritual. City rivalry does the rest.

11 — RISKS

What breaks, and the answer

RiskAnswer
Male supply too thin → no matches → women leaveThis is the real danger. 300 men against 100 women is 1:3, which is fine — but only if all 300 are active. Cap the queue at 3× the female count and refresh weekly. Never let the ratio drift past 1:5.
Coin holders hoard or resell codesCodes are bound to her account and revocable. One code, one man, one time. If a code surfaces in a WhatsApp group, revoke it publicly — the enforcement is itself good marketing.
A man is vouched for and behaves badlyHer code is the leash: three reports against a vouched man revokes his seat and costs her a code. Social accountability, not moderation queues. This is how small towns already work — you're just formalising it.
Event turns into a "tax on loneliness"Real failure mode: women paying ₹12,000 for a dinner with three men they'd never swipe on. Rules — women never pay, the room is women-only, no one is pitched anything, and anyone can leave without explanation.
Family finds out and it escalatesDisguise mode, no surname, no contacts sync, no "friends of friends" graph, and no notification content that reveals intent. DPDPA-compliant data minimisation throughout — no Aadhaar images, ever.
The coin becomes a joke / gets counterfeitedSerial numbers are registered to accounts. A coin that isn't in the registry is worthless. Counterfeits become a story about how real the thing is.
